Flexion of the sheathed flagellum

In situ structural microbiology

Visualizing cellular nanomachines across resolution scales.

By integrating high-throughput, high-resolution imaging with structure-guided mutagenesis and functional analyses, my lab will connect the native molecular states and spatial organization of chemosensory arrays to bacterial motility and environmental adaptation. Beyond chemotaxis, this platform will enable systematic analysis of diverse molecular machines, linking native molecular architecture to cellular behavior and physiological function. Together, these studies will establish general principles by which bacteria sense and respond to environmental signals while providing a biological and technological framework for applications ranging from antimicrobial development to biomanufacturing.

Research program

A structural view of bacterial behavior

My research asks how bacterial nanomachines convert environmental signals into coordinated movement and physiological adaptation. I combine in situ structural imaging with genetics, live-cell microscopy, and quantitative functional analysis to move from native architecture to biological mechanism.

Scientific directions

From molecular structure to cellular function

01 / Architecture

Nanomachines in native cells

Resolve intact flagellar motors, filaments, and signaling assemblies while preserving membrane contacts, molecular interactions, and cellular context.

02 / Mechanism

Motility and signaling

Determine how sensory inputs, molecular switches, and force-generating complexes coordinate bacterial navigation, adaptation, and host colonization.

03 / Scale

Near-atomic cellular maps

Advance high-throughput in situ cryo-EM and cryo-ET toward cellular maps that connect molecular structures with their positions and functions.
